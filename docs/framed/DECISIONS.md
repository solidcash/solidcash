# Per-call decisions ledger

The single place an item's **current status** and its **authorization** live. Other framed documents cite a row
id from here instead of restating a decision; when a decision changes, its row is edited in place and the row it
replaces is marked `SUPERSEDED-by <id>`. A status sentence anywhere else is a pointer, not a decision: if the two
disagree, this file is right and the other one is stale.

What is checkable here is the **provenance** column: it names where each answer came from, so a reader can tell an
authorization from a paraphrase instead of having to adjudicate one. Nothing mechanical enforces the convention -
an earlier round added a test that grepped the documents for superseded phrasings, and it was removed as work whose
subject was prose rather than behaviour.

## What each live row decides, and what it does not

- **D5** decides PW3's status: met on its own Done-when (the three refusals gone from the published census, the
  master tests passing, the published facts in the ladder stream's hands), with the fixture's `updateJobWhitelist`
  shape - a varying-key loop upsert whose loop body also calls `publishJobEvent`
  (`.marketplace/contracts/MarketplaceV1.sol:474-497`) - handed to the ladder stream as a pinned reproducer. The
  loop protocol itself is served and is this lane's landing: one to four turns settle through the shipped path in
  any key order, each with a refused record mutation. D5 does **not** claim the fixture's own entry publishes a
  write-segment fact; it does not, because the write's segment is decided by a call inside the loop body, which is
  the hop's callee-frame planning.
- **D4** decides that PW7's chunk-output row certifies the shipped encoders' arithmetic and that the composed
  reading stays owed, with the blocker named (the read-modify-write of one long value in one frame).
- **D1** and **D3** are the ladder stream's and the plan's standing directions, unchanged by this lane's work.
- **D24** decides the form of an `address` *value* at the two surfaces that turn it into bytes - a bare address in
  `abi.encodePacked`, and the presentation of a decoded address - and settles that the *casts* are not part of the
  choice: `uint160(a)` and `bytes20(a)` truncate and `address(x)` is a no-op in **both** forms, so no declared width
  can lie. It does **not** decide the form of an address in transit (that is D29), the `msg.sender`
  physical-vs-logical question, or the basis on which addresses order (`<`, `>`), which stays the compiler's
  existing value comparison. What holds it: `packages/solidc/test/Casts.test.ts` (the casts truncate in either form;
  the bare address packs whole under `'bytecode'`) and `packages/solidc/test/AddressFormIntrospection.test.ts` (the
  form reaches the packing of `msg.sender`, `tx.origin` and `address(this)`; it does not reach address equality, the
  zero comparison, an event address field, or the `ecrecover` pairing - and `uint160(msg.sender)` is byte-identical
  in either form, which is how a Solidity-shaped comparison stays expressible under `'bytecode'`).
- **D29** decides the form of an address *argument* on the framed wire, and it is **taken: the wire carries the
  lock**. The earlier reading of this row - that the wire was a fixed-width word the SDK fed the identity - was wrong
  for *arguments*: `encodeFunctionArgument` hands an `address` its locking bytecode, the master's entry body reads
  the raw push, and the only narrowing left in the call-site binding is the digest's identity projection for a value
  read back from a Solidity ABI result (D13). What actually kept the title red was that the SDK could not *learn*
  the form the compile chose: the artifact dropped the `addressForm` option, so `artifactAddressForm` re-derived
  `'hash'` from `evmCompat`, and the SDK's emulation of `abi.encodePacked(<address>)` (three sites) packed the
  20-byte identity where the body packed the lock. Both ends are fixed and the title is green. The one fixed-width
  lane that remains, and is *not* this decision, is the message-bus kernel's graph/argument **word** (a 32-byte ABI
  word cannot hold a 35-byte lock); that is the same width argument D24 records for a word-encoded result, and it
  still governs a word lane.
- **D30** decides against adding the batch-width binding on the evidence evaluated at `3dc7e8b54`:
  - The direct/kernel witness is used as an output offset without an equality check against the actual lane width.
    Read-only chunk counts come from input commitments; the framed convention derives its batch width in-script.
  - The proposed event-suppression shape was a loop emit sharing a fixed emit's output after a negative offset.
    The hostile test exercised a different, two-row kernel batch: every tested deflated or inflated width was
    already refused before the patch. That result neither proves nor refutes the proposed fixed/loop alias.
    A multi-row event-forgery hypothesis also remained unconfirmed. No funds or state exploit was established.
  - The candidate binding added 20 bytes in the measured route and exposed two honest SDK paths needing changes:
    compose can resolve a predecessor index after fixing the width, and the direct external-call builder can
    publish an insert count while those inserts still share one placeholder index. Landing requires reconciling
    those paths, checking affected byte-size pins and repeating the integration checks.
  - The user evaluated that evidence and cost and chose the current behavior. The retained branch is reference
    material, not queued implementation work. Revisit only as a new evaluation, for example after a reproducer or
    a relevant protocol change; the decision does not establish that every event layout is safe.
  - Status 2026-10-19: the branch was merged regardless (`370ed3716`, 2026-10-15) and the binding ships on
    `master`. Asked whether to revert it, the user ruled "if D30 is beneficial, we should not revert". It is kept: it
    takes a spender-chosen offset out of every trailing coordinate, which is defense in depth whether or not a
    reproducer exists, for 72 to 76 bytes on the direct BchNative tokens.
- **D50** decides the direct convention's result binding: a call reads the record its callee pinned for exactly that
  call, and a callee run admits no other record of its category. It does **not** bind what the direct convention
  cannot run in one spend: a fixed callee repeated in a loop, or called twice with different arguments, goes to the
  atomic composer, as before; two master threads of one callee in one transaction are refused by the count. An opaque read (`call`,
  Multicall-style loops) checks no digest: its result is bound by the exact count and by D8's argument binding.
  R11's view witnesses stay caller-permissive, a loop iteration's result must fit 96 bytes (no per-iteration
  preimage), and a returning call into another proxy stays unsupported (`UpgradesDeployProxy`, skipped).
